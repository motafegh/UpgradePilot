# UpgradePilot Chronicle

This folder is the informal, subjective, and collaborative story of the UpgradePilot journey.

It exists to preserve experiences that ordinary project artifacts may not capture: how ideas appeared, what happened around the work, what Ali was thinking or feeling, how conversations changed direction, why an unexpected module emerged, what seemed important at the time, and anything else Ali chooses to include.

## Nature of the chronicle

The chronicle is voluntary and human-led.

- Ali decides whether, when, and how anything is recorded.
- No entry is required after a session, milestone, decision, or event.
- Entries may be written immediately, reconstructed later, left unfinished, or never written.
- Content may be technical, personal, reflective, incidental, only partly related to UpgradePilot, or apparently unrelated.
- Entries may be incomplete, subjective, uncertain, contradictory, imprecise, or factually mistaken.
- The aim is to preserve the journey as experienced and remembered, not to create an authoritative reconstruction.
- No fixed format, template, length, chronology, naming convention, or writing style is required.

This folder may contain prose, notes, reconstructed stories, conversations, reflections, images, links, or any other form that suits a particular entry.

## Authority and project relationship

The chronicle is context, not control.

Nothing in this folder:

- changes project scope, plans, specifications, architecture, implementation, status, or authorization;
- proves that an event, decision, capability, or result occurred exactly as described;
- needs to be cited, synchronized, promoted, or referenced by another project artifact;
- becomes an action item merely because it appears here.

When an idea from the chronicle later matters to formal project work, it may be independently examined and placed in the appropriate plan, specification, ADR, source, test, or other owning artifact. That promotion is separate from the journal entry itself.

Formal project artifacts may be used as context while creating an entry, but the chronicle does not need to follow their structure, terminology, evidence requirements, or documentary style.

## Authorship and AI assistance

Ali is the primary author and owner of the chronicle.

AI must not create, schedule, infer, or maintain entries automatically. AI participates only when Ali explicitly asks it to help with a particular entry or reflection.

When requested, AI may:

- lightly edit Ali's writing while preserving his voice and intended meaning;
- reconstruct a readable narrative from Ali's explanation;
- add relevant details from project materials when Ali asks for them;
- help recover chronology or surrounding context;
- provide a separately identifiable reflection;
- jointly develop an entry with Ali;
- draft a complete entry from the account Ali provides.

AI assistance should preserve uncertainty, emotion, contradictions, informal details, and unresolved endings where they belong. It should not automatically turn an entry into a progress report, decision record, lesson list, success narrative, action plan, or polished portfolio artifact.

An entry may optionally state how it was produced—for example, written by Ali, lightly edited by AI, reconstructed by AI from Ali's account, or written as a joint reflection—but this is never required.

## Preservation

Past entries do not need to be corrected or rewritten merely because later understanding changes. A later reflection may add a different interpretation while leaving the earlier account intact.

This folder is allowed to remain sparse, irregular, inconsistent, and unfinished. Those qualities are not defects; they are part of the record it exists to preserve.
