# Whole-project product and design audit plan

## Objective and deliverable

Audit what UpgradePilot delivers to a public Python repository maintainer, how its implementation supports that delivery, and which observed gaps should drive the next investment. The audit covers active product code and public entry paths, accepted design, evidence correctness, engineering verification, operational delivery, and product value. The [Charter](../PROJECT_CHARTER.md) defines the product promise. The [90-day route](UPGRADEPILOT_90_DAY_PLAN.md) defines milestone expectations. The [documentation map](../docs/README.md) locates the accepted contracts and architecture decisions.

Deliver one formal, dated report in [`audits/`](../audits/README.md) with an executive verdict, a product and evidence-flow map, coverage and verification status by responsibility, prioritized findings, reproducible evidence references, and a ranked assessment of future AI opportunities. Keep detailed execution receipts in the dated audit working memory or its evidence directory. Keep follow-up selection in [`MEMORY.md`](../MEMORY.md).

## Scope and safeguards

Account for every active product package and public entry path in the coverage map. Inspect normal producers, integration, and reachable consumers. Use representative traces for repeated mechanisms and deepen inspection where behavior, proof, or risk differs. Include tools, experiments, historical material, and project controls when they affect product delivery, interpretation of proof, or a decision the audit must make.

The audit may read retained evidence, run controlled local verification using frozen inputs, and write its report, evidence, and handoff records. Keep product code, tests, configuration, specifications, ADRs, and target repositories read-only. Fresh provider acquisition, inference, hosted workflow dispatch, external study or outreach, and target-code execution require separately selected scope. Follow [`SECURITY.md`](../SECURITY.md) for private data, credentials, and untrusted inputs. Publish public-safe findings, references, and hashes. Keep private captures and raw logs at their authorized retention boundary.

## Pin the inspected baseline

1. Reconcile local and remote `main`, record the exact inspected commit and tree, and identify any out-of-baseline work. Inspect or run against that baseline in an isolated checkout or source export where necessary. If main advances, keep the original horizon explicit and recheck affected findings before claiming that they apply to the later revision.
2. Record the operating environment, interpreter, dependency set, installation method, and available local evidence relevant to verification. Apply [`ENVIRONMENT.md`](../ENVIRONMENT.md) recheck rules. Bind every retained proof used for a current claim to its product revision, profile, input identities, and affected code or method.
3. Use [AUDIT-012](../audits/2026-10-22_AUDIT-012_whole-project-value-capability-and-delivery.md) as the prior whole-project comparison. Reconcile each material finding against current owners and evidence. Use narrower audits for specific provenance or closure questions. Record whether each old concern persists, changed, was resolved, or needs new proof. Inspect the current product across its full scope even where Git history shows no recent change.

## AI engineering opportunities

Map where UpgradePilot already uses a large language model, under what conditions the model runs, which outcomes were actually observed, and where models, retrieval, and agent workflows remain research. Begin with the [adopted bounded support-drop extractor](../docs/architecture/ADR-0006-bounded-local-support-drop-semantic-extractor.md), the [Investigator evaluation plan](INVESTIGATOR_INTEGRATION_AND_COMPARATIVE_EVALUATION_PLAN.md), and the [completed broader interpretation comparison](../working-memory/evidence/2026-10-19-investigator-evidence-first-build/COMPARISON_REVIEW.md). Trace the model seam from admitted evidence through attributed output, validation, and report effect. Carry forward negative results and identify the changed premise a successor would test. Use the [existing AI design checkpoint](UPGRADEPILOT_90_DAY_PLAN.md#evidence-grounded-ai-design-checkpoint) for admission and evaluation.

| Candidate seam | Question for the audit |
|---|---|
| Bounded extraction and interpretation | Where does natural-language meaning in release notes, PR descriptions, source, or CI error text materially limit a supported maintainer task? Test the case for a tightly specified, source-linked proposal against the simplest appropriate parser, metadata check, or current report. |
| Evidence retrieval and navigation | Do relevant sources already exist but take material effort to find? Compare direct source selection and lexical search with semantic retrieval over revision- and version-filtered evidence. Distinguish missing acquisition from poor ranking and measure which decision-relevant evidence each method retrieves or misses. |
| Evidence-grounded explanation | Could a model help explain a verified finding, surface competing hypotheses, or phrase uncertainty for the maintainer? Compare with deterministic rendering of the same structured record. Review source-to-claim support and whether the output preserves identity, scope, material caveats, and the difference between evidence and action permission. |
| Bounded tool use and investigation planning | Identify a real case where a fixed evidence path leaves a decision-critical question unanswered. Assess whether constrained selection of existing read-only evidence tools could improve the next observation or stopping decision. Compare with a fixed procedure and inspect admissible tool scope, resource ceilings, termination, and behavior on incomplete evidence. Consider an agent architecture only if the task demonstrates a need for that coordination. |
| Verification and developer assistance | Evaluate whether assisted review, failure triage, or experiment analysis could reduce a concrete engineering burden. Record the human or executable check that can validate each contribution. Treat generated judgments and model agreement as proposals, including when a second model acts as a reviewer. |
| Model integration and operations | Check seams for typed outputs, source references, model and prompt identity, local deployment, request and runtime budgets, explicit failure and abstention, and reproducible evaluation. Report infrastructure opportunities only where a candidate has a specific reliability, privacy, cost, latency, or maintenance need. |

For each credible opportunity, describe the feature as a user or engineering workflow. Specify what triggers it, the decision and evidenced gap it addresses, its integration layer, the admissible inputs, and the proposed output, validation owner, and fallback. Record the simpler baseline, prerequisites, and expected benefit or cost. Describe an evaluation with held-out or contrasting cases, false-claim and omission checks, failure and abstention behavior, and measured time or cost where relevant. Identify the data and trust boundary, including treatment of untrusted instructions in source text, and keep source identity, evidence admission, tool authorization, and action permission under their accepted owners. Rank candidates as ready for a bounded design or evaluation, dependent on a named prerequisite, lower priority than a simpler method, or unsupported by the current evidence. These dispositions guide later selection under existing plans.

## Inspection and verification sequence

1. **Map the shipped system.** Follow declared entry points into orchestration and enumerate each branch, its activation conditions, output, evidence owner, and explicit failure or unsupported state. Map active packages to responsibilities. Mark claims exposed only in developer diagnostics, experiments, or future plans using their own proof status.
2. **Trace claims and adverse inputs.** Trace material maintainer-facing claims backward to the source and forward through the owner boundary. Follow contrasting missing, failed, malformed, stale, or mismatched inputs forward to the visible result. Sample every coverage row and deepen when evidence is contradictory, a trust boundary changes, or a finding could affect the recommended priority.
3. **Reuse retained cases by proposition.** Start with [MapProxy matrix acceptance](../working-memory/evidence/2026-11-21-matrix-final-acceptance/RESULTS.md), the [cross-profile activation matrix](../working-memory/evidence/2026-11-13-cross-profile-activation-matrix/ACTIVATION_MATRIX.md), and the [maintainer next-check review](../working-memory/2026-11-21_maintainer-next-check-review_lbd-cycle.md). Use MapProxy for command evidence and variant recovery, ApplyPilot for failed consumption versus cause and declared conflict, OpenCV for publication versus target applicability, Pydantic/Soup Sieve for conditional model activation, and AV-Tracer for exact historical source/CI provenance. Keep live, frozen replay, modified controls, and saved-reader evidence distinct. Add an existing contrast only when an obligation remains untested.
4. **Run focused checks, then broader verification.** Select checks against the threatened contract and its normal entry path. Include controlled reader/recovery or source-to-report checks when delivery is at issue. Then run the maintained deterministic product regression on the pinned baseline, verify the [experiment dependency boundary](../tools/check_experiment_boundaries.py), and check the isolated installed package and both CLI entry points using available local installation inputs. Use retained proof when equivalent and identified. Record which checks ran afresh, their commands, environment, outcomes, and relevant limitations. Distinguish current local verification from historical hosted or live-provider results. If a necessary check is blocked, record the exact reason, affected claim and re-entry condition. Diagnose failures within the review boundary and preserve reproducible evidence for any repair.
5. **Reconcile and rank.** Compare current observations with the prior audit, supported product outcomes, and existing plans. Determine the earliest sufficient owner for each gap. Evaluate the simplest credible response and what proof would justify it. Rank by current user consequence, trust and correctness impact, evidence strength, and cost or dependency of the next step. Compare the AI opportunities with deterministic alternatives and other product improvements, including the proposed peer-pin consistency check.

## Finding and proof standard

For each material finding, record the claim, exact revision and reachable path, applicable owner, evidence and counterevidence, user or engineering consequence, and classification. Use reproducible source or behavior evidence for confirmed discrepancies. Describe a design risk with the affected premise and the observation that would discriminate it. Identify intentional support limits, planned capability, and open verification separately. Assign stable finding IDs for later references. Include retention recommendations where removing or rewriting established behavior could lose evidence or compatibility.

For every coverage row, state what was inspected, the evidence type and horizon, and what remains unresolved. Direct source traces, controlled tests, installed checks, and retained exact-case evidence support different claims. An earlier passing test suite or exposed developer comparison does not establish a fresh live success, general compatibility, or independent maintainer usefulness. Mark incomplete checks and absent proof explicitly, including their consequence for confidence. Use the [audit reporting guidance](../audits/README.md) for finding and evidence structure.

## Completion and handoff

The audit is ready when the formal report covers each active responsibility and public entry path, each material finding has traceable evidence and a proportionate disposition, the prior findings are reconciled, and verification limits are visible. Include a ranked AI opportunity assessment with prerequisites, simpler alternatives, and evidence needed before adoption. Recheck the inspected revision, references, reproducibility record, scope claims, and public-safe content before publication. Report the recommended next decision, the strongest competing option, and what evidence would change the ranking.

Close the audit after recording the report and evidence and reconciling the live handoff. A finding may inform a later plan, specification, ADR, repair or evaluation through its normal owner. An audit recommendation carries no automatic authorization for that work.
